## Section 2 — Purpose of AI Review

The AI is being used as **Second marker** and **Feedback assistant**.

The AI should assess the essay holistically — evaluating argument, use of evidence, structure, and referencing. It should not assess the question's factual content beyond what is standard for a first-year psychology essay.

### 4b — Rubric

**Criterion 1 — Quality of Argument (15 marks)**

- **Full (13–15):** Clear thesis, sustained argument, evidence integrated from sources, counter-arguments addressed, conclusion supported.
- **Partial (8–12):** Clear thesis, some evidence, argument inconsistently sustained, counter-arguments mentioned but not developed.
- **Low (3–7):** Weak thesis, limited evidence, descriptive rather than argumentative.
- **Zero (0–2):** No discernible argument, unsupported assertions, or off-topic.

**Criterion 2 — Use of Evidence (10 marks)**

- **Full (8–10):** Sources integrated critically, evidence supports each claim, sources are academic and appropriate.
- **Partial (5–7):** Sources used but not integrated, evidence occasionally supports claims, some sources non-academic.
- **Low (2–4):** Minimal use of sources, evidence does not support claims.
- **Zero (0–1):** No evidence used.

## Section 13 — Feedback Style and Sample Feedback

**Detail level:** Extended prose.

**Sample feedback:**

> "Your essay presents a clear thesis on Piaget's stages of cognitive development, and the structure follows a logical progression from introduction to conclusion. However, the argument is inconsistently sustained in the middle sections — at times the essay becomes descriptive rather than argumentative, listing stage characteristics without analysing their significance. Your use of evidence is strong in the opening paragraphs, where you integrate Vygotsky's work effectively, but weakens towards the end. The reference list is complete, though two sources (a blog post and a Wikipedia article) do not meet the academic standard expected for a first-year essay."
